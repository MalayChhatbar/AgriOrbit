# Crop calendars & weather sensitivity (India)

## Kharif vs rabi seasons
Kharif crops (paddy, maize, cotton, soybean, groundnut) are sown with the onset of the
southwest monsoon (June-July) and harvested September-October; they depend directly on
monsoon rainfall. Rabi crops (wheat, mustard, gram/pulses, barley) are sown October-December
on stored soil moisture or irrigation and harvested March-May; they are sensitive to winter
frost and to heat during grain filling. Zaid (summer) crops like watermelon and vegetables
run March-June and need assured irrigation.

## Wheat
Wheat needs cool weather during tillering (13-20 °C) and dry, bright weather at harvest.
Rain during grain filling or harvest causes lodging and grain discolouration; if heavy rain
is forecast near maturity, harvest early at physiological maturity. Night frost below 4 °C
during flowering damages the crop — light evening irrigation before a frost night buffers
canopy temperature.

## Rice (paddy)
Paddy tolerates ponding; transplant when at least 100 mm of monsoon rain has accumulated and
the 7-day outlook stays wet. Maintain 3-5 cm standing water, but drain beyond 7 cm after
storms to avoid submergence stress. Avoid fertiliser application within 24 h of heavy rain —
it washes away.

## Cotton
Cotton is drought-hardy but hates waterlogging: 3-day rainfall above 90 mm risks square
(bud) shedding. Bollworm pressure rises in humid, overcast spells — scout twice weekly in
such weather. Picking should avoid rain days; wet kapas yellows and fetches lower prices.

## Pulses and oilseeds
Pulses (gram, arhar) and mustard are drought-tolerant but very sensitive to frost and to
wet spells at flowering, which spread fungal disease. Groundnut needs calcium and dry
weather during pegging/harvest; rain during digging makes pods sprout in-shell.

## Vegetables
Vegetables are the most weather-sensitive common crops: they need frequent light irrigation
rather than rare heavy irrigation, suffer blossom drop above 35 °C, and develop fungal
disease within 48 h of leaf wetness. Stake tomatoes/chillies before windy storms.

# Irrigation & safe field operations in changing weather

## When to irrigate
Irrigate when the forecast 7-day water deficit (ET₀ minus expected rain) exceeds ~20 mm, or
when the 3-9 cm soil-moisture layer trends down for 5+ days in the growing season. Water
early morning or evening to cut evaporation losses by up to 30%. Never irrigate immediately
before heavy rain — it wastes water and waterlogs roots.

## Water-saving practices
Alternate furrow irrigation saves ~30% water versus flooding every furrow. Mulching crop
residue cuts evaporation and keeps the 0-1 cm layer cool. Laser land-levelling reduces ponding
hot-spots. Drip kits for vegetables and orchards save 40-60% water and allow fertigation.

## Spraying weather rules
Spray only when: no rain expected for 6+ hours, wind is 5-15 km/h (below 20 km/h), and
temperature is below 32 °C. Early morning is usually ideal. Never spray before storms —
runoff contaminates water and wastes chemical. For any dose decision, follow the product
label and consult the local agriculture officer (Krishi Vigyan Kendra).

## Heatwave operations
During heat alerts (≥38 °C), irrigate standing crops early morning, avoid transplanting and
spraying, shift fieldwork before 11 am and after 4 pm, and keep drinking water available for
everyone working in the field.

## Frost operations
When night lows approach 4 °C: irrigate fields the previous evening (wet soil releases heat),
avoid deep hoeing, and if possible generate smoke/smudge at field edges overnight. Frost
damage is worst on clear, calm nights following a sunny cold day.

## Harvest & storage
Use multi-day dry windows for harvesting and sun-drying; grain must dry below 12% moisture
before bagging. If wet weather interrupts, spread produce thinly under cover and turn it
daily; never bag warm wet grain — aflatoxin risk rises quickly.
